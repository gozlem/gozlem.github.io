Our research was featured in [UCF News](https://www.ucf.edu/news/ai-based-screening-method-could-boost-speed-of-new-drug-discovery/) highlighting the development of **AttentionSiteDTI**, an AI-based drug screening process to accelerate medicine development.

The model uses natural language processing techniques to represent drug-protein interactions as words corresponding to binding sites, employing deep learning to extract interaction features. It is the **first interpretable model using protein binding site language**, achieving up to **97% accuracy** in identifying promising drug candidates.

The team developed a self-attention mechanism that learns which protein regions interact with drug compounds, enabling drug designers to identify critical binding sites and functional properties essential for drug effectiveness. Testing included SARS-CoV-2 spike protein compounds relevant to COVID treatment research.

The study, published in *Briefings in Bioinformatics*, was led by doctoral student Mehdi Yazdani-Jahromi with co-authors Niloofar Yousefi, Aida Tayebi, Elayaraja Kolanthai, Craig Neal, and Sudipta Seal. The research was funded by UCF's internal AI and big data seed funding program.
